Callers supply observed page captures, graph nodes and sitemap documents. Canonical declarations preserve source and ordinal; graph walks retain each observed URL and distinguish cycles, scope, failures, unknowns and limits. Alternate declarations retain their source, ordinal, field entry index and state. Only valid alternate declarations become graph edges; duplicate occurrences remain distinct. Alternate analysis describes observed clusters, with missing reciprocity and self-declarations dependent on collection completeness. Caller-provided discovery completeness describes only the supplied observation and does not establish that a crawl or the web is complete.

Sitemap parsing accepts supplied XML or gzip bytes under byte and entry limits. Entries retain their document, ordinal, raw location and lastmod, and parsing errors. Lastmod syntax is not a measurement of freshness. Origin traversal returns bounded document paths, each index-hop ordinal, and the leaf entry ordinal; missing, invalid, out-of-scope, cyclic, omitted, mismatched-source or limited evidence makes coverage incomplete. Page observations distinguish blocked, redirect, errors, noindex, noncanonical and unknown. A clean observed path is conditional on the supplied documents and observations, not proof of global discovery or intended indexability. `enforce_document_budget` preserves a prefix and records omissions; its byte budget excludes any enclosing envelope.

Scope here is the analyzer's host-based HTTP(S) check, with explicit nondefault ports excluded; callers must choose suitable scope and retain actual source and discovery state. The analyzers do not fetch, crawl, persist, schedule, emit wire payloads, establish provider indexing or infer policy intent. Run-level discovery, robots and sitemap coverage, shared budgets, durable checkpoints and historical freshness remain separate work. No public receiver contract or S03 issue completion is claimed.
